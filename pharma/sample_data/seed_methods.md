# Brand performance decomposition
## Purpose
Explain a brand's sales movement by decomposing it into market growth, share change, and price/mix effects, so the team addresses the actual driver instead of the headline number.

## When to use
Any "why did sales go up/down" question about a brand with at least 8 quarters of TRx/NRx and revenue data.

## Inputs required
Brand and market TRx (or units) by period and geography; net or gross revenue by period.

## Step-by-step method
1. Compute market growth: what the brand would have done at constant share.
2. Compute share effect: (share change) x (current market volume).
3. Compute price/mix: revenue change not explained by volume.
4. Rank the three components by absolute contribution; lead the narrative with the largest.
5. Drill into the largest component by geography/segment to localize the driver.

## Watch-outs
Never compare TRx across markets with different average days-supply. Normalize TRx by market growth before calling anything a "decline" — a flat brand in a growing market is losing.

## Output format
Waterfall chart (market growth / share / price-mix), one-paragraph narrative naming the dominant driver, table of top 5 geographies by contribution.

# Regional TRx decline triage
## Purpose
A fast, ordered checklist to localize the cause of a regional prescription decline before anyone books field-force time.

## When to use
A region or district shows a TRx decline vs. prior period materially worse than the national brand trend.

## Inputs required
TRx by region by period (weekly or monthly); if available: call activity, formulary status changes, competitor launches by region.

## Step-by-step method
1. Confirm the decline is real: compare against the same region's seasonal pattern and the national trend, not the prior period alone.
2. Check data artifacts first: reporting lag, wholesaler buying patterns, panel changes in the data vendor's sample.
3. Check access: any formulary tier change, PA imposition, or plan exit in that region in the window.
4. Check competition: launches or promotion surges localized to that region.
5. Check execution last: vacancy, call-volume drop, targeting changes.

## Watch-outs
Steps are ordered by base rate: most "declines" are artifacts or access changes, not rep performance. Do not skip step 2.

## Output format
One page: trend chart of region vs. national indexed to 100, the checklist with each step marked ruled-out/confirmed, and the single most likely driver with confidence.

# Launch uptake tracking
## Purpose
Judge whether a new product's uptake is on-track without waiting for full quarters of data.

## When to use
First 26 weeks post-launch of any product, or when benchmarking a competitor launch.

## Inputs required
Weekly NRx/TRx from launch week; analog launches in the same class or an adjacent class with comparable access.

## Step-by-step method
1. Index weekly NRx to launch week and overlay 2-3 analog launch curves indexed the same way.
2. Track NRx-to-TRx conversion from week 8 onward as the persistence signal.
3. Track breadth vs. depth separately: writer counts vs. scripts per writer.
4. Flag deviation from the analog corridor for two consecutive weeks as a trigger for driver analysis, not a single week.

## Watch-outs
Free-goods/bridge programs inflate early NRx; state which channel the data reflects. Analog choice drives the whole read — justify it on access comparability, not therapeutic similarity alone.

## Output format
Uptake curve vs. analog corridor, writer-breadth chart, one-paragraph on/off-track call with the trigger rule stated.
